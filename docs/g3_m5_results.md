## Why does averaging gain more than any input? (POST-HOC; explanation only, not used for selection)

The plain average is M1 + (Δ_M2 + Δ_M3)/3, where Δ is an input's correction to M1. A post-hoc scan
on the registered out-of-fold predictions (scratch script; not a registered run) gives ΔP1 vs
m1_E_resB0 for M1 + c·Δ:

| c | 0.25 | 1/3 | 0.5 | 0.75 | 1 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| M2 correction only | +0.0053 | +0.0066 | +0.0082 | +0.0079 | +0.0034 |
| M3 correction only | +0.0034 | +0.0040 | +0.0046 | +0.0039 | +0.0018 |
| both corrections | +0.0081 | +0.0094 (= m5_avg) | +0.0100 | +0.0051 | −0.0058 |

- Both corrective stages are **over-scaled by about 2×**: half the M2 correction alone is worth
  +0.008, against +0.0034 for the full correction.
- The M2 and M3 corrections correlate only 0.30 across cells, so the average also combines two
  partly independent signals.
- The averaging gain is therefore mostly shrinkage of over-confident per-gene corrections, not a new
  signal. A plausible cause: the per-gene stages are fitted on inner-OOF M1 predictions (models
  trained on 80% of the training lines) but applied to the full-fit M1. That is a mismatch in the
  scale of the base predictions.
- Picking c = 0.5 from this table would be post hoc and is **not** proposed. The average was
  pre-declared as the M5 baseline, and its weights are fixed (1/3), so its +0.0094 is an honest
  out-of-fold number.
- It is also the simplest available form of the shrinkage.
- The "M2 / M3 no-go" verdicts of D19/D20 were correct for those rungs as fitted. The corrective
  signal was real but mis-scaled.

Why the stacker does not beat the average:
- Its NNLS weights for variable-selective genes put about 0 on M1 and about 0.55/0.45 on M2/M3
  in 4 of 5 folds (fold 0: 0.30/0.01/0.68), so it mostly does not shrink the corrections towards M1.
- Its inner-level inputs (M2/M3 fitted on 80% of the training fold) evidently do not show the
  outer-level over-scaling the same way, and NNLS minimises squared error, not per-gene r.
- Stacking costs 3 h 40 min against minutes for the average, and adds nothing measurable.

## Permuted-input control: pre-stated expectations missed, interpreted

The control was m5_stack with the m2n_E_paralogs predictions row-permuted across lines, in both
the weight-learning data and at prediction. Two pre-stated expectations failed.

**(a) Weight on the permuted input: max 0.041 over 20 class × fold cells (expected ≤ 0.01).**
- The mean weight is 0.010.
- The bound was mis-specified. One permutation is shared by all genes, so any line-level structure
  common to many genes enters every gene the same way. An example is the overall
  dependency/screen-quality level of line π(i), against y of line i.
- The spurious alignment therefore varies on the scale 1/√(n lines) ≈ 0.035, not
  1/√(n cells) ≈ 0.001.
- Two post-hoc seeds (configs m5_ctl_perm_s1/s2, committed before running) gave max 0.035 and 0.037,
  means 0.011 and 0.010. The weights are chance-level and stable across permutations, and they
  never approach the weight the unpermuted input gets (0.49–0.62 in the variable
  common-essential / selective classes).
- Read this way, the control shows what it was meant to show: NNLS does not pick up an
  uninformative input beyond chance.

**(b) P1 ≥ m5_stack − 0.002.** P1 was m5_stack − 0.005.
- The expectation assumed m2n_E_paralogs is redundant given M1 and M3. It is not: the post-hoc
  scan shows its correction is the larger of the two.
- This is informative about the inputs, not a malfunction of the stacker.
- Even with M2 removed, the control (≈ stack of M1 and M3) scores +0.0048 over the headline,
  above M3's own +0.0018. That is the M3 shrinkage effect again.

Neither miss bears on the selected model. m5_avg has no learned weights, and its predictions equal
the mean of the three registered input runs' predictions to 3e-7 (float32 caches).

## λ-criterion diagnostic (D20c): outcome

- On the headline path the LOO-r criterion does not suffer from the small-λ artefact in practice.
  It chose λ 21,544 (variable) and 46,416 (other) in every fold, never the grid floor of 100.
- Grouped inner-CV r chose **the same λ for variable genes in all 5 folds**. For other genes it
  chose λ 46,416 (2 folds) or 100,000 (3 folds).
  - P1 variable is identical to 4 decimals (ΔP1 −0.00005).
  - P1 on non-variable genes is −0.0005 (0/5), and P1 all is 0.1542 vs 0.1547.
- LOO-MSE chooses more shrinkage in both groups: 46,416 for variable genes and 215,443 for other
  genes, in all folds. It is uniformly worse:
  - P1 variable −0.0011 (1/5);
  - non-variable genes −0.0037 (0/5);
  - P2b −0.0074.
- By gene-SD decile (training-fold SD, all eval genes), neither alternative helps the weakest
  genes:
  - in deciles 0–4, ΔP1 is within ±0.002 for both;
  - the losses grow with SD (deciles 5–8: LOO-MSE −0.003 to −0.013, inner-CV r −0.0003 to −0.003);
  - decile 9 is about the variable genes.
- **Verdict: the suspected small-λ bias of LOO r on weak-signal genes is not present on real data.**
  - The noise artefact (LOO r → −1 as λ → ∞) matters only when a group has essentially no signal,
    as in the permuted control.
  - LOO r stays the criterion of record; D20c is closed as robustness only.

## Deviations and provenance

- **D-M5-1 (declared in the config before running):** fully nested inner OOF for M2 needs a third
  level of M1 fits (about 650 fits) and projected over 5 h together with M3.
  - So the M2 inner fits chose their local λ by the non-nested inner-fold CV of G2
    (nested_lambda=False) inside the inner-training lines.
  - That is leak-free with respect to the inner-held-out lines; the independent review confirms
    this.
  - All 25 inner M2 fits chose local λ 100, against 31.6 for the outer nested M2, so the weights
    were learned on a somewhat more shrunk M2.
  - M1 inner OOF came from the exact-key cache cache/m2_inner. M3 inner fits were exact
    (nested_es=True).
- **Time box:** the 5 h box held.
  - Fold 0 took 45 min, so the pre-declared fallback m5_stack_f1 (55 min trigger) was not needed
    and was never run.
  - m5_stack wall time was 3 h 40 min (12:29–16:10); m5_avg and the control added 6 min, and the
    post-hoc seeds 6 min.
  - Per outer fold: inner M2 OOF ≈ 10–12 min, inner M3 OOF (incl. the nested-ES M1 OOF passes) ≈
    19–28 min, outer refits ≈ 4–9 min.
  - Peak RSS 3.5 GB.
- **Post hoc, labelled as such:** the c-scan of corrections and the control seeds s1/s2. Neither
  is used for selection.
- **Independent leakage review** of the stacking nesting (a fresh agent that did not write the code):
  - It found **no leakage**. Inner OOF rows come only from fits without those lines, and M1 cache
    reuse is exactly keyed.
  - No held-out ID reaches any fit, weight, class or cache key. A launcher-level perturbation of
    held-out targets changed M5 predictions by ≤ 4e-16.
  - Its minor findings, acted on in commit 0e02b23:
    - `predict` now refuses training-fold lines;
    - the F1 config text was corrected;
    - tests were added for predict-time permutation, the 0.01 bound, `check_nesting` negatives,
      NaN targets and the cache round-trip/exact keys.
  - Not done, and stated here:
    - M5's internal input fits bypass run.py's Sentinel/FIT_LOG (safe by construction and covered
      by the toy tests);
    - cache keys do not include code or library versions;
    - the split check against cache/m2_inner recomputes the same splitter rather than reading the
      splits stored with the file.
  - The guard was added after m5_stack started. It changes no output; m5_avg and the controls ran
    with it.
- Every run has `tree_clean = 0`: another agent's G4/G6 files were untracked or being committed
  concurrently. `dirty_tree = 1` for m1_E_resB0_icvr and the post-hoc seeds; the tracked
  modifications at launch were report/config-generator scripts, not model code. Runtimes are
  indicative (shared machine, LESSONS 14).
